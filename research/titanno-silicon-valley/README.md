# TITANNO: Silicon Valley Prospect Brief

**Compiled:** October 7, 2026 · **Companion spreadsheet:** [`TITANNO_SV_Prospects.xlsx`](./TITANNO_SV_Prospects.xlsx) (sortable, includes LinkedIn search links, suggested ask per prospect, and status/meeting columns for tracking)

**Positioning used throughout:** TITANNO is a research, development, engineering and manufacturing organisation for civil unmanned autonomous systems (UGVs and UAVs) covering logistics, inspection, infrastructure, public safety and industrial automation. It is seeking customers, technology partners, and investors for an international manufacturing and R&D base in Japan or South Korea.

---

## 1. Key takeaways before you fly

1. **Your strongest pitch is the manufacturing problem in Silicon Valley robotics.** The leading Bay Area robot companies are scaling fleets fast:
   - Serve grew its fleet ~20x in 2025.
   - Starship plans to quadruple its fleet by 2027.
   - DoorDash is starting to make its own robot, *Dot*, in Fremont.
   - Knightscope's K7 moves from Beta to deployment in Q4 2026.
   - Matternet just raised $33M to launch a next-generation drone.

   These companies are software- and AI-strong but short on hardware production capacity. "We engineer, industrialise and build your robot, and give you a launch base in Japan/Korea" is a concrete offer that answers a real need.
2. **For drones, the US market is now regulated by place of manufacture.** Since Dec 22, 2025 the FCC has barred new equipment authorisations for *all* foreign-made drones and drone critical components. Temporary exemptions run to Jan 1, 2027 for Blue UAS-listed products and US "domestic end products" (US-made with ≥65% US content). In practice:
   - A Japan/Korea-built UAV cannot easily be sold in the US.
   - Use the Japan/Korea base for UAVs aimed at Asia, Europe and other export markets.
   - Pursue US UAV business through partnerships or US-located final assembly.
   - Ground robots (UGVs) are not covered by this rule.
3. **The Japan/Korea base is a selling point for the investors.** Japanese and Korean corporate VCs in Silicon Valley (Yamaha Motor Ventures, Toyota Ventures, Hyundai CRADLE, LG Technology Ventures, Samsung Catalyst) belong to groups with factories, robotics businesses, and national industrial-policy goals. They are more likely to back a Japan/Korea manufacturing base than a pure US VC would be.
4. **Keep every conversation civil.** Several targets (Skydio, Applied Intuition) also have defense businesses. Stay on inspection, logistics, public safety and industrial use. That avoids export-control and investor-screening friction on both the US and Asian side.
5. **Use JETRO and KOTRA first.** Both have Silicon Valley offices and can produce a concrete incentives comparison before you meet investors. Candidate sites: Fukushima robotics zone and subsidies in Japan; Daegu robot cluster with land and investment support in Korea. A plant plan with real numbers makes the investor meetings much stronger.

## 3. Outreach approach

- **Prepare one-pagers before outreach, three versions:** (a) for robot OEMs: "manufacturing and engineering partner + Asia launch base"; (b) for technology partners: "integration and co-development"; (c) for investors: "global civil-autonomy manufacturing platform in Japan/Korea".
- **Cold-email formula:** one line on TITANNO's capability, one line on their specific trigger event (use the "Evidence" column), and a specific 30-minute request on named travel dates.
- **Proof points to bring:** current product portfolio, production capacity and quality certifications (ISO 9001; AS9100 for UAV work if held), reference customers, and an outline of the proposed Japan/Korea plant (capex, timeline, headcount).
- **Contacts:** names are publicly listed executives as of this research. Senior executives rarely take cold meetings, so try to reach them through SVR, JETRO/KOTRA, or investor introductions. Otherwise, use the business-development or partnerships contact on each company's website. Confirm every title on LinkedIn before outreach.

## 5. Caveats and next steps

- **Verify before outreach:** company situations in robotics change quickly; acquisitions, pivots and leadership changes are common. Items marked "verify" in the spreadsheet were not confirmed by a 2026 source. Examples: Zipline–Toyota Tsusho status, the PG&E program owner, and Yamaha Motor Ventures fund status.
- **Not included on purpose:** defense-led companies; firms that have wound down or pivoted away from hardware; and large lists of generic VCs. The list favours prospects with a specific, current reason to talk to TITANNO.
- **Gaps to fill with TITANNO input:** home country and current certifications; product specs and price points; production volumes; preferred host country (Japan or Korea). These would let the list be re-ranked and the outreach emails drafted.
- **Events:** check the [SVR robotics events calendar](https://www.svrobo.org/robotics-events/) for your travel dates. Bay Area robotics meetups and investor sessions run most weeks.
